UX + visual language

Print-industrial system for Amerimatter: aged paper field, deep ink structure, vermilion only for critical calls. Catalog-clear hierarchy so stock and order paths read as transactional and premium.

Project
Amerimatter
Type
Design post · draft
Audience
PMs · UX · product
DESIGN PRINT_INDUSTRIAL CATALOG BRAND

Target audience

CNC shops, buyers, and plant-facing operators who need stock, cut, and order information without marketing fog. Surfaces must scan like a catalog: clear SKUs, hard structure, immediate next action.

Design choices

  • Stratified type — bold all-caps sans for headers; serif for body and descriptive copy.
  • Flat structure — hard borders, no soft shadows; alignment and order over decoration.
  • Limited palette — paper field, ink for structure, slate for meta, vermilion only for DO / critical CTAs.
  • Catalog metaphor — listings, contents, price or quote, requisition-style actions where the journey is commercial.

Palette

Paper
#F4ECE2
Ink
#111213
Navy
#1D2D3D
Slate
#8BA0B1
Vermilion
#DF3A23

Paper is the field. Ink carries body and structure. Navy anchors primary navigation and controls where needed. Slate is secondary technical meta. Vermilion is reserved — danger, material finish callouts, and critical transactional CTAs — not decorative accents on every chip.

Goals

  • Make industrial commerce readable: stock → product → cut/plan → order.
  • Feel premium and transactional without looking like generic SaaS.
  • One system across catalog, product plate, forge demo, and guides.
  • Design decisions documented so brand life stays reproducible in portfolio work.

Personas / workflow

CNC buyer

Needs alloy, size, finish, and price path — catalog and product plate first.

Shop planner

Cut and nest before purchase — forge / cutter demos prove craft and reduce waste questions.

Brand owner

Needs a coherent surface across marketing, catalog, and commerce — not three different aesthetics.

Product maturity signal

Design choices lock brand identity before platform scale: print-industrial honesty, catalog structure, and commerce-ready CTAs. Interactive demos prove craft; the stack follows the design system, not the other way around.
