Growing meat in the kitchen

A biohacker’s DIY cultivated meat experiment — turning fertilized-egg cells into tissue-like mass using kitchen-adjacent reagents. Educational only. Do not eat the product. Inspired by open methods in the Shojinmeat tradition.

NOT FOR EATINGBSL hygiene~$200–5002–3 weeks

01 · Why

  • Curiosity about tissue engineering
  • Hands-on cell culture skills
  • Touch the future of food at garage scale

Disclaimer: experimental / educational. Biosafety practices required. Do not consume.

02 · Materials

  • Fertilized chicken egg
  • Dry ice, small centrifuge, incubator
  • Yeast extract, fresh egg whites, konjac powder
  • Sterile dishes, pipettes, 70% ethanol, gloves

03 · Process

  1. Incubate fertilized egg 10–12 days at 38.5°C, high humidity
  2. Extract embryo sterilely; freeze (dry ice), mince, centrifuge cells
  3. Media — 1–2% yeast extract + 10–20% egg white; pH ~7.3; sterilize
  4. Proliferate in dishes at 38.5°C; swirl daily; growth often 3–7 days
  5. Scaffold — konjac gel for 3D structure (not just monolayer)
  6. Mature & harvest another 7–10 days → small tissue-like masses

Timeline ~2–3 weeks. Yield is microscopy-scale, not dinner.

04 · Safety & troubleshooting

  • Sterility is the #1 boss fight
  • Temperature and pH religiously
  • Egg white can help as a mild antimicrobial in media
  • Dispose as bio-waste
  • No growth → egg freshness / pH; cloudy media → contamination, restart

05 · Next experiments

Different media, fish cells, better scaffolds, or more advanced techniques once fundamentals are solid. Industrial cultivated meat is not a kitchen hobby — this is skill-building and curiosity.

Design bias

Learn the biology. Never skip sterility. Never eat the experiment.

Takeaway

The tech that may one day feed cities is already legible enough for careful home practice — with clear ethical and safety bounds.
