Defense of Vanilla JS

On dependency gravity, bundle size, and the quiet power of the platform you already shipped to a billion devices.

PlatformZero-buildConstraints

01 · OpeningWhen leverage becomes rent

There is a moment in every long-lived product when the framework stops being leverage and starts being rent. The upgrade path lengthens. The mental model multiplies. A “simple” button imports half a design system and three polyfills for APIs the browser already owns.

02 · ScopeNot a crusade

This is not a war on React, Vue, or Svelte. It is a defense of choice — specifically, the choice to ship less when the problem is small, stable, and already solved by the web platform.

03 · CostWhat you do not invoice

Dependencies cost install time, review surface, CVE noise, and the attention of whoever inherits the repo. In mission-critical paths — auth, payments, offline recovery — every extra abstraction is a place for silent failure.

“The best dependency is the one you never had to pin, patch, or explain.”

04 · PlatformWhat vanilla still does better

  • Fetch, history, custom elements
  • CSS variables, container queries
  • View Transitions, form validation
  • Kilobytes of intent, not megabytes of habit

05 · When frameworks winScale is real

Large teams, design systems at scale, highly interactive product surfaces — frameworks earn their keep. The mistake is defaulting to them for every URL.

Field note

This site’s page wipe uses a single canvas-free View Transition script and no bundler. The transition works because the problem was small enough to own end-to-end — not because a framework made it possible.

Takeaway

Start with the platform. Graduate when the complexity is real, not imagined. Vanilla JS is not nostalgia — it is fewer moving parts, clearer ownership, and a bundle measured in intent.
